# MEMO — Informal eviction, the mark, migration tracking, Hernandez (fetched 2026-07-14)

## 1. Informal/illegal eviction
- Desmond & Shollenberger 2015, Demography 52(5):1751-1772, doi:10.1007/s13524-015-0419-9 (abstract PubMed 26286885: "more than one in eight Milwaukee renters experienced an eviction or other kind of forced move in the previous two years"). Split verified via Desmond, IRP Fast Focus 22-2015 p.3: forced moves = 48% informal evictions, 24% formal, 23% landlord foreclosure, 5% condemnation. 2:1 in print: Gromis & Desmond 2021 Cityscape 23(2) ("'informal evictions' are twice as common as formal"). National (2017 AHS, same source): informal 72.3% vs formal 13.1% of forced moves = 5.5:1, WITH authors' caveat AHS undercounts formal (0.8% vs 2.3%). [Demography body paywalled — split via IRP brief.]
- Garboden & Rosen 2019, City & Community 18(2):638-661: "landlords avoid costly evictions, instead relying on the *threat* of eviction. By redefining renters as debtors, filing assists in rent collection"; "In Baltimore ... about 6,500 evictions are executed per year, while landlords *file* ... approximately 150,000 times" (working paper: https://www.law.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/Garboden_Rosen_ThreatofEviction_2019.pdf).
- Balzarini & Boyd 2021, "Working With Them," Housing Policy Debate 31(3-5):425-445 (read: https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/Working-With-Them-Small-Scale-Landlord-Strategies.pdf): "You pay them, cash for keys..."; "Off-the-books evictions are evictions nonetheless ... not recorded and thus are not recognized in official eviction rates." (71 interviews, Philadelphia — sample size via publisher/Urban summary.)
- LA lockouts: Keep LA Housed, We're Not Going Back (5/10/2022, https://www.saje.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/KLAH-Report_Not-Going-Back.pdf): "24,699 evictions ... filed between March 2020 and March 2022 ... 13,780 lockouts between March 2020 and September 2021 (LASD data)" — data "collected and maintained by Kyle Nelson" (fns. 2, 9); p.9 harassment incl. "utility shut offs, and/or illegal lockouts ... an estimated 500 or more tenants experience landlord harassment in the city [of] Los Angeles each month" (citing Cantong, USC NDSC 2/28/2022). Nelson peer-reviewed: Lens, Nelson, Gromis & Kuai 2020, City & Community, doi:10.1111/cico.12487.
- Self-help illegal: Main 2022, 43 Cardozo L. Rev. 2205: "nearly every state has abolished the use of self-help and permits landlords to evict tenants only through judicial proceedings." https://www.cardozolawreview.com/an-unqualified-prohibition-of-self-help-eviction-providing-a-right-to-court-process-for-all-residential-occupants/
- LSC Eviction Laws Database = right cite for ladder-varies-by-state ("the entire legal process of eviction – from pre-filing to post-judgment").

## 2. The mark
- Kleysteuber 2007, 116 Yale L.J. 1344 (read: https://yalelawjournal.org/pdf/539_3abmt4eo.pdf): reports "recite any eviction action filed, regardless of whether it is still pending or who prevailed" (p.1346 n.5); NYT screening-founder quote: "policy of 99 percent of our [landlord] customers ... flat out reject anybody with a landlord-tenant record, no matter what the reason ... or outcome" (p.1347).
- Porton, Gromis & Desmond 2021, Housing Policy Debate 31(3-5):377-394, doi:10.1080/10511482.2020.1748084: 3.6M records, 12 states — "22% of eviction records contain ambiguous information on how the case was resolved or falsely represent a tenant's eviction history."
- Benfer 2025, "Dismantling the 'Scarlet E'," 86 Ohio St. L.J. 1081 (official PDF): "the moment an eviction is filed, it creates a permanent public record—a 'Scarlet E'" (p.1084); rent-collection filings still mark tenants "indefinitely" (p.1084); "Nine out of ten landlords ... rely on third-party tenant screening" even if "dismissed" (pp.1085-86); industry "$4.5 billion in revenue in 2024, up from $1 billion in 2020," 2,000+ companies (p.1081); "Twenty states and the District of Columbia have adopted eviction record-sealing laws, ... eighteen ... active as of September 2025" (p.1086 n.20).
- Filing-vs-judgment gap: Hepburn, Louis & Desmond 2020, Sociological Science 7:649 (via Benfer p.1085): Black renters filing 6.2% vs judgment 3.4%; white 3.4%/2%.

## 3. Filings != displacement
- Gromis et al. 2022 PNAS: 69.7M filings 2000-2018; 3.6M/yr; ~2.7M households/yr (~1.33 filings/household); "Court records do not capture whether a tenant ultimately vacated the property ... requires matching court records with supplementary data recording address changes."
- Collinson, Humphries, Mader, Reed, Tannenbaum & van Dijk 2024, QJE 139(1):57-120 (Census WP text https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2023/adrm/ces/CES-WP-23-37.pdf): Cook County — "Approximately 65 percent of eviction cases ... end with an eviction order ... Sheriff's Office executes about 26 percent of cases ending with an eviction order" (≈17% of filings executed); NYC — "about 35 percent of nonpayment cases end with an eviction order ... 31 percent ... result in an enforcement" (≈11%); ~64% of NYC non-order cases settle. Causal (PMC10772599): order -> +3.4pp emergency shelter use; +8.2pp new address at 1 yr; concentrated among Black and female tenants.
- Treglia, Byrne & Rai 2023, Housing Policy Debate, doi:10.1080/10511482.2023.2186749 (NLIHC summary): +1pp filing rate -> +0.205/10,000 sheltered homelessness next year; "no evidence of a relationship between eviction judgments ... and homelessness" — FILINGS are the predictor.
- HPS scale: Fusaro et al. 2025, Socius 11 (n=783,832; behind-on-rent 19% Jan 2021 -> 11% Mar 2024; persistent Black/Hispanic disparities). Urban Institute (Reynolds & Burton 11/3/2023, corrected analysis): 44% of renter households felt pressure to move in past 6 months; among pressured, 9% threatened w/ eviction; 42% of threatened left; "69 percent of renters whose landlords changed the locks or turned off utilities" left.
