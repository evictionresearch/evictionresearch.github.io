Free and open source

Our tools.

Eviction records sit in thousands of separate court systems, and few of the organizations that need them can afford to build software to read them. ERN releases the tools behind its research as free, open-source code. Courts, legal aid groups, agencies, and other researchers can use them to collect and analyze eviction data themselves. Anyone can read the code to see how our numbers are made.

Python and R · AGPL-3.0

extract-text Under development

extract-text turns eviction court filings, including scanned and handwritten pages, into a table of cases with party names, addresses, and case details. It runs entirely on your own computer, so records never leave your hands. It was validated on more than a million pages of Washington State filings.

Visit the site → R package · MIT

neighborhood

The neighborhood package holds R functions for studying neighborhood change with census data. They include racial typologies of census tracts, housing affordability by income level, and the interactive maps and charts used in ERN's state eviction profiles.

Research for social good

What we do.

The Eviction Research Network collects, analyzes, and maps eviction data and helps other researchers do the same. We use social and data science to measure racial and gender disparities in eviction, and urban theory to explain them.

We study how common eviction is and what drives it in regions with little research. We point readers to other scholars' work. We give legislatures, practitioners, and advocates evidence they can use.

Keeping People in Their Homes: the impact of emergency rental assistance on evictions

This neighborhood-level study tests whether $46.6 billion in emergency rental assistance reduced eviction filings. It covers 700+ counties and 12,000+ census tracts in 22 states.

Washington eviction filings, 2016–2026

Over the past 38 months, 1 in 44 renter households faced an eviction filing. That is roughly 52% above the pre-pandemic normal. Nine of 39 counties exceed the state rate.

Toward a national eviction data collection strategy using natural language processing

ERN read 111,740 Washington eviction court records from scanned PDF and TIFF images. It then estimated the racial and gender disparities in filings that county-level counts do not show.

For groups and classes

Evicting the American Dream.

Documentary · 2025 · 51 minutes

Evicting the American Dream tells the stories of children and families facing eviction and homelessness. The filmmakers also interview pediatric psychologists, social workers, tenant advocates, housing officials, and eviction lawyers. The film traces the causes of eviction and argues that eviction can be prevented.

Counter Stories Productions, the film arm of Presbyterian Disaster Assistance, made the film. The producers make it available to schools, organizations, and communities that want to host a screening. They also publish a resource guide for the discussion afterward.
