# MEMO — Baltimore vs King County filing counts + mechanism (verified 2026-07-14)

## Baltimore City volume
- Justice Diverted (Public Justice Center w/ Right to Housing Alliance, Pasciuti/JHU, Cotton/UB), Dec 2015, https://abell.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/cd-justicediverted216.pdf (PDF read):
  - p. iv: "6,000 to 7,000 renter households are judicially evicted [annually] ... a court system ... overwhelmed by landlord litigation, to the tune of 150,000 rent cases annually."
  - pp. 4-5: ">600 rent complaints each day ... roughly 150,000 complaints and about 70,000 warrants" annually; early 1990s "upwards of 195,000"; 1.26 cases per renter household (2000), 1.25 (2013); fn.11: 155,376 complaints in 2013 (Sheriff's Office data). Fig. 2 series 2000-2015 (160,995 in 2000 ... 151,416 in 2015).
  - pp. 9-10: "6,880 evicted households per year on average since 2012 ... one in 17 renter-occupied housing units."
  - p. 6: filing->warrant "two to three weeks"; service by mail + door posting; "trial by the fifth day after filing." Reform #1 (p. v) asks for a pre-filing notice — confirming none existed (2015).
- Md. Judiciary FY2017 Statistical Abstract, Table DC-2 pp. 37-38: District 1 (Baltimore City) LANDLORD-TENANT FILED = 144,058 (statewide 650,348; PG 154,841; Balt. Co 168,896). https://www.mdcourts.gov/publications/annualreport/reports/2017/fy2017statisticalabstract.pdf (caveat: all L-T filings, but FTPR is "far and away" the bulk + "single largest docket ... in Maryland's" courts per Justice Diverted p.4).
- Network for Public Health Law fact sheet (Soloway et al., May 2021), p.1: ~140,000 annual filings vs ~125,000 rental units; only ~6,500 (4.6%) end in removal (citing Stout 2020 p.*19 — Stout primary 404'd, secondary-only flag). Also: "$15" lowest filing fee (with DC). https://www.networkforphl.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Fact-Sheet-Deterring-Serial-Eviction-Filing.pdf
- Baltimore Banner (Kasakove/Little/Thieme, 8/31/2022): "Maryland landlords filed 671,000 evictions in 2019 — roughly 56,000 a month"; ~23,000/month H1 2022. https://www.thebanner.com/community/housing/for-some-maryland-landlords-filing-for-eviction-is-a-monthly-routine-tenants-pay-the-price-HSUABD736VAUZOKX7VZDTR2L3A/

## Mechanism (filing-as-notice; serial filing)
- Gromis et al. 2022 PNAS (verbatim): "In Maryland, eviction lawsuits can be filed immediately following nonpayment of rent for relatively low cost ($15 to $25); no prior notice is required from the landlord." + "creates an incentive for landlords to enforce rent collection by using filings as a threat of eviction." + "Repeated filings against the same households constituted 57.4% of all cases in Maryland" + MD "a seemingly impossibly high filing rate of 69.6% in 2018." National: "Between 2000 and 2018, 69.7 million eviction cases were filed ... more than 3.6 million filings annually"; ~2.7M households/yr, "just fewer than 7 in 100 renting households." https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9173767/ (PNAS 119(21) e2116169119)
- 2021 HB 18 -> Ch. 746, enacted 5/30/2021, EFFECTIVE 10/1/2021: RP §8-401(c)(1) 10-day written notice of intent to file, cure bars filing. Enrolled chapter: https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2021RS/chapters_noln/Ch_746_hb0018E.pdf
- Garboden & Rosen 2019, "Serial Filing: How Landlords Use the Threat of Eviction," City & Community 18(2):638-661, doi:10.1111/cico.12387 (127 landlords, Baltimore/Dallas/Cleveland): "landlords generally try to avoid costly evictions, instead relying on the serial threat of eviction. By redefining renters as debtors, filing assists in rent collection..." https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cico.12387
- Leung, Hepburn & Desmond 2021, "Serial Eviction Filing...," Social Forces 100(1):316-344, doi:10.1093/sf/soaa089 (8,108,674 records, 28 states, 2010-2016): "Of the 1,082,845 evictions filed in 2014, 44.6% were associated with serial filings"; 30.4% of households filed against multiple times at same address; ~$180 fines/fees per filing = +20% monthly housing cost; median refiling 2.4 months; 88% within a year. https://academic.oup.com/sf/advance-article/doi/10.1093/sf/soaa089/5903878 ; Eviction Lab post 9/15/2020: https://evictionlab.org/serial-eviction-filings/
- Eviction Lab FAQ (verbatim): "Because of how Maryland recorded eviction notices prior to 2021, it has a much higher filing rate ... In most jurisdictions, the eviction process starts with an out-of-court notice ... but in Maryland the process used to begin with an eviction filed in court. Many landlords filed against their tenants every month ... modified in October 2021..." https://evictionlab.org/help-faq/

## Flags
