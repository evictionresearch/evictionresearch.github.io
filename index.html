Displacement is rarely one event. We measure it.

The Eviction Research Network is the academic arm of CiDR Lab, housed in UC Berkeley's Department of Sociology. We study what happens where housing, health, criminal-justice contact, and climate exposure meet — and build the open data infrastructure that lets researchers, agencies, and advocates act on it.

Plate I · The Risk Model

Risk, mapped to neighborhoods where no data exists.

The Housing Precarity Risk Model is a national forecast of where renters face the greatest combined risk of eviction, displacement, and long-term poverty. Where court records are sparse, the model fills them in; where they are dense, it ranks neighborhoods by severity.

HPRM v2.0 is stewarded by ERN and CiDR Lab, dual-branded with the Urban Displacement Project. Policymakers, legal-aid providers, and government agencies use it to target rental assistance, design anti-displacement zoning, and prioritize eviction-defense resources.

Plate II · The Apparatus

Court records → tracts → demographics. One method, 37 states.

Pipeline stages

Every ERN state profile runs the same pipeline. Civil-court records are sourced from five data partnerships, geocoded to census tract, name-cleaned to exclude businesses, demographically estimated by Bayesian inference, and deduplicated. The full method is documented in the 2024 Cityscape paper and reproducible from open code.

  1. 1

    Acquisition

    Partner with five data sources covering 30+ states — LSC, PSU, Chicago Legal Aid/ACLU, Baltimore Sheriff, WA OCLA.

  2. 2

    Geocoding

    Address → latitude/longitude → census tract, with controlled redistribution for low-precision rows.

  3. 3

    Demographic estimation

    Bayesian ecological inference (BISG / fBISG / birdie) on surname + neighborhood — never assigned, always estimated.

  4. 4

    Aggregation

    Deduplicated case counts aggregated to tract, county, state, race, and month — backbone of every profile and the HPRM.
